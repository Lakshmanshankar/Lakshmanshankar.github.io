Dear Admissions Committee,

I completed my Bachelor of Science in Computer Science in 2023, where I developed a broad foundation in computer science and was introduced to the principles of software engineering through coursework on software development methodologies, software testing, and engineering practices. These courses gave me an understanding of how software should be designed and developed systematically. However, it was my subsequent professional experience that made me appreciate the relationship between software engineering and the people who ultimately use the software.

After completing my degree, I joined a small startup as a Developer Relations engineer. Although my role involved working with software and developers, it also placed me in direct contact with users and their problems. This gave me an important perspective that I had not developed during my undergraduate studies: software can provide the functionality users need, yet users may still struggle to accomplish their goals because they cannot easily discover or navigate that functionality. Understanding the difference between what software is capable of doing and what users are actually able to accomplish with it became increasingly interesting to me.

I have encountered a similar problem in my current work at an enterprise software company. Working closely with customer-facing aspects of the product has reinforced my observation that many difficulties are not necessarily caused by missing functionality. Instead, users can sometimes have difficulty finding the appropriate options, understanding the available workflows, or navigating through a complex application to achieve a particular goal. This made me increasingly interested in the question of how software interfaces can be designed to make existing capabilities more accessible to users.

The emergence of AI has made this question particularly interesting to me. I began considering whether natural-language interaction could provide an additional interface to existing software functionality. Rather than requiring a user to understand the structure of an application and navigate through multiple menus, forms, and settings, an AI system could interpret the user's intent and invoke the appropriate capabilities within the application.

I am currently exploring this idea through a web publishing platform that I have developed independently, Pagewrite. Instead of treating AI as a separate chatbot, I am experimenting with exposing the application's existing operations as structured tools that an AI system can invoke. For example, a user could describe a desired configuration in natural language, provide the required information, and allow the system to perform the corresponding operations within the application. The objective is not to replace the existing user interface, but to provide another means of interacting with the same underlying software capabilities.

This practical experimentation has led me to a broader academic interest: how can software engineering approaches be used to design applications that are easier for users to understand, navigate, and operate, particularly when AI becomes an additional interaction layer? I am interested in studying how such systems can be evaluated in terms of usability, task efficiency, reliability, user control, and the ability of users to effectively discover existing software capabilities. I would like to understand not only whether this approach is convenient, but also the engineering principles and design processes required to integrate it responsibly into real software systems.

This is the primary reason I am seeking a Master's degree in Software Engineering. My undergraduate education provided me with the foundational knowledge of computer science and software engineering, while my professional experience has exposed me to the practical challenges that arise when real users interact with software. I now want to bridge these two perspectives through deeper study of software engineering processes, software architecture, quality assurance, human-centred software design, and modern approaches to developing software systems.

I believe that a master's programme will also provide me with the research methodology needed to turn the questions I have encountered in practice into systematic investigations. In particular, I would like to explore how AI-assisted interaction can complement conventional graphical user interfaces and whether it can meaningfully reduce the difficulty users experience when navigating complex software. This interest could potentially develop into a master's thesis evaluating different approaches to AI-mediated interaction with existing software workflows.

I am particularly motivated to study in Finland because of its strong emphasis on technology, research, and practical education. An international academic environment would allow me to learn from different perspectives while strengthening my ability to work on complex software engineering problems. I also see the programme as an opportunity to move beyond implementing individual features and develop a deeper understanding of how robust, usable, and maintainable software systems are designed.

In the long term, I want to work as a software engineer building reliable and user-centred software systems, while continuing to explore how emerging technologies such as AI can improve the way people interact with software. My professional experiences have shown me a problem that I want to understand more deeply; my independent work has allowed me to begin experimenting with a possible solution; and I see graduate study in Software Engineering as the next step toward studying that problem rigorously.

I believe that this combination of academic foundations, professional experience with users and software systems, and independent experimentation gives me a clear motivation for pursuing graduate study. I look forward to developing these interests further through the Master's programme and contributing my practical perspective to its academic environment.
