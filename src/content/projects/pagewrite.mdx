That's also a common experience, and it comes down to how your meal is balanced.

A bowl of oatmeal by itself is often **high in carbohydrates but relatively low in protein and fat**. It digests fairly quickly, so you may feel hungry again after 2–3 hours.

For example:

- 50 g oats + milk:
   - ~250–350 kcal (depending on the milk)
   - ~10–15 g protein
   - Low fat (unless whole milk or nuts are added)

Compare that with:

- 3 eggs + 2 slices of whole-grain bread:
   - Similar calories
   - 20–25 g protein
   - More fat
   - Often keeps people full longer.

### Satiety is influenced by three main factors

1. **Protein** (the biggest contributor)
   - Eggs
   - Greek yogurt
   - Cottage cheese
   - Tofu
   - Lean meat
2. **Fat**
   - Nuts
   - Peanut butter
   - Seeds
   - Butter
   - Avocado
3. **Fiber**
   - Oats
   - Whole grains
   - Vegetables
   - Fruit

Oatmeal has plenty of fiber, but if it's missing enough protein and fat, it may not keep you full for long.

### If you wanted oatmeal to be more filling

Instead of:

- Oats + milk

Try:

- Oats + milk + 2 boiled eggs on the side
- Oats + Greek yogurt + nuts
- Oats + peanut butter + chia or flax seeds
- Savory oats with eggs and vegetables

Adding protein and healthy fats can make a noticeable difference in how long you stay satisfied.

### But you don't have to eat oats

There's a common misconception that oatmeal is the "ideal" breakfast for everyone. In reality, the best breakfast is one that:

- You enjoy.
- Keeps you full until lunch.
- Fits your nutritional needs.

Many people simply don't feel satisfied after oatmeal, while others can go 5–6 hours without feeling hungry.

Given what you've described—**bloating and hunger again by around 11 AM**—oatmeal with milk may just not be the best breakfast for you.

If your goal is to build muscle, stay focused at work, or lose fat while staying full, a **higher-protein breakfast** is often more effective. For example:

- Eggs with whole-grain toast
- Idli or dosa with a protein-rich sambar
- Vegetable omelette with toast
- Greek yogurt with fruit and nuts (if you tolerate dairy)
- Leftover rice with dal and vegetables

In fact, a traditional South Indian breakfast like **idli with sambar** or **dosa with sambar** can be surprisingly balanced: the fermented batter is generally easy to digest, and the lentils in the sambar add protein and fiber, often making it more satisfying than a plain bowl of oatmeal.
